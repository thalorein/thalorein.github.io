Raucuous cheers erupted through the arena as spectators cheered for the duels in the pit. The Coliseum was Ul'dah's worst kept secret. Once a place used only to pit slaves against each other, it had now become something of a sport, with all sorts of duelists coming to pit their mettle, be it against fellow men or against exotic creatures. Its violent & bloody practices famously earned it the fitting nickname "the bloodsands". Many came far and wide to watch such matches, and betting on a winner was the pasttime of many Ul'dahns.

F'ynn was yet young. At a mere eight summers, he peered over the barricades almost weekly to watch the fights, his deep red eyes always fascinated by the whirl of techniques thrown around the ring. *If only I could fight like that,* he often thought to himself. His favourite by far was watching a tanned highlander who wore a bull-like helmet fight in the ring. This fighter was feral, yet he moved with a grace unseen in any other opponents he had watched - wild yet controlled, every strike precise and never careless. He fought like a raging sandstorm, and any opponent who had been caught in it were catapulted off their feet and into the dirt, left breathless and unable to continue. The crowd would always erupt in cheers. *It was as if he was born for the theatrics of the arena.*

But one day, he noticed something else about this fighter. He'd suddenly gained a helper - a small lalafell with shoulder length silver hair. The kid didn't even look that much older than him. Curious indeed, he thought as he watched the kid help this fighter with his gear. He'd spent enough time around the Coliseum to recognize that many duties here were often carried out by those enslaved, especially those who couldn't yet fight for their keep in the arena.


--

"Father", he'd heard the kid call as he walked past the pair once, wind touseling the strands of his sky blue hair. That was certainly something he wasn't expecting. It'd been a year since he'd seen the kid around the Coliseum. What was the history behind it? 

He didn't have to ponder long. There was soon huge news about this highlander buying over the entire coliseum, and in a short time, single-handedly earned himself a seat on the Syndicate. And the most atrociou or even outrageous news he heard? The Sultana herself making a public declaration of pardoning this fighter on a whim! She hadn't made much impression before then, having never made any bold decisions under the scrutiny of public eye - courtesy of being put on the throne at such a young age with probably no experience with politics - but clearly this highlander struck him as someone who had made a big enough impact in her life for her to do something so drastic. Impact enough to affect the life of two very different kids. It's a rare enough thing to see in Ul'dah for sure.

Putting the pieces together, he finally arrived at one conclusion: this fighter had bought the freedom of the young boy by his side. What other explanation could there be?

--

At twelve summers, F'ynn made a major decision for the first time in his life - he joined the Gladiator's Guild. No one bat an eyelid - it was common for kids of this age to have started working or at least training to become an apprentice of some sort. He could have become a merchant like his parents, but he chose to shun his lot in life. Something about the highlander, his son, and his impact with the Sultana was by now, deeply ingrained in him. And with that, he felt the urge to follow a different path in life.

Was he making the right choice? Who knows. But instinct called to him, and he heeded its call.

--

**Four years later...**

F’ynn stared at the leaflet handed hastily to him as he left yet another one of his matches at the bloodsands, throwing his winnings into his pouch as he left the victor for the day. It was a recruitment notice for the Immortal Flames. Unsurprising, after the seventh umbral calamity had recently upended most of their lives, and on top of that, and there was also the sudden renewed prospect of war with the Garleans looming over their heads. *They'd need all the manpower they can get*, F'ynn thought practically.

But at the same time, he wondered whether this was a twist of fate. He'd known that the highlander, Raubahn, was the head of the Immortal Flames. There weren't many things he trusted in this capital of seemingly glittering riches, where lies, flattery and deceit were commonplace, but something told him that Raubahn was someone he could follow without regrets.

He seriously considered the offer in his hands. By any standard, this was a rather lucrative job, despite its risk. To add on to the fact, joining was also easy as the grand company was in dire need of people to fill its many empty ranks, having lost most of its personnel during the catastrophic Battle of Carteneau against the elder primal, Bahamut. 

Not to mention, the rise of Vesper Bay had all but siphoned all business opportunities away from the Silver Bazaar, resulting in most of the merchants losing their livelihoods. Even his family hadn't been spared. Its much larger and open port made it much easier for huge merchant ships to dock and easily trade good with Ul'dah, which meant, who would want to make the tedious effort to load cargo bit by bit between a small row boat and a large vessel into the tiny and shallow waters of the Silver Bazaar? 

Who knew that in just four short years, life could change so drastically? 

An unwelcome memory intruded his thoughts - his family, like most of the other residents of the Bazaar, had clung to the past, the ways of glory. The inability to move forward with times. Literally leaving them in ruins. As if living in an arid desert wasn't already bad enough. They'd lost even the smallest meagre of comforts, with the baggage of no one being meaningfully employed except probably for him. A sour taste sprang up in his mouth, and he gritted his teeth. *No more thinking about this*, he thought as he made up his mind then and there. Money was all that mattered. It was idealistic to believe that anything could be done without moving any sort of gil in a place like Ul'dah.

He headed for the Hall of Flames, the headquarters of the Immortal Flames, with the recruitment poster tucked under his arm. There was some paperwork to complete - but one question gave him pause: what name to use on the contract? He sat in thought for a few seconds, weighing his options. Then, in a sudden flash of inspiration, he had it. Without any more hesitation, he wrote it down. The contract was signed shortly afterward, following a brief and cursory interview with someone whose name he only vaguely recalled as Swift Ryder.

He was then ushered into a room to get his measurements taken, and also to meet the Vice Commander for an introduction to the official commands of the Immortal Flames. 

The moment he locked eyes with the Vice Commander, he instantly recognised who it was. The same tanned skin, silver hair and black eyes. The only difference being, the shoulder length hair had now grown longer.

“Hey there, F’ynn, yes? A strange name for a Dunesfolk, but nevertheless, thank you for joining our ranks” , the tanned young adult smiled warmly. “The name's Pipin. Glad to have ya here.” 

F'ynn could only give a small smile and nod in return.
