> Aftermath of "In Crimson it Began"
{: .note }

Kotaro blinks, confused by the scenery in front of him. There's a barren wasteland stretched out for yalms. He has zero clue on where he's going, or what he's doing, but something compels him to trudge through it. And so he does. But as he walks, strange things start to happen. The sky suddenly darkens, and the ground on which he walks starts to feel more like liquid than solid. His legs start to feel heavier and heavier, as if weighed down by lead. Finally, he dares to look down, expecting to see quicksand. But to his horror, what he sees is a white pile of souls and skeletons, some gripping onto his shoes.

_"Warrior of Light, why couldn't you save us?"_

He quickly looks back up, determined to sprint to escape the grasp of the Netherworld, but the visage of a bulk of armor now stands before him. Abruptly, he freezes.

"_Your friends were a disappointment. But you... You will entertain me, will you not_?"

Zenos' haunting words echo in his head as he jerks awake, having fallen asleep by a giant tree, on a small cliff by the river outside Castrum Oriens, relieving the events of the day before in a twisted dream. Cold sweat drips down the side of his forehead.

Everything had seemed fine, until it had all gone terribly wrong - Rhalgr's Reach was not supposed to be infiltrated. But it had, and the night ended in a massacre, with half of the Resistance forces decimated, and the hideout left in flames. And the worst of it all? The knowledge that they were cut down by none other than their own fellow Ala Mhigans and the Imperial Viceroy himself. The fires of war knew no bounds.

Everyone, most especially Conrad, had been devastated. For despite the bitter truth of what happened that day, these people were still their flesh and blood. An Ala Mhigo won and freed without including them could not truly be Ala Mhigo at all. But how many of the populace would truly forgive their atrocities? The flames of hatred are after all, not truly easy to extinguish.

Kotaro heaves a world weary sigh.

He had been attempting to expand his repertoire of skills to become more all-rounded since the ravages of the Dragonsong war.

It had started with picking up the ways of a knight, which hadn't come easy, given his lack of propensity to guard against attacks. But he figured that he needed to learn how to do it if he wanted any chance of drawing more attention to himself so others could escape.

More recently, through a chance encounter with a lost job stone in the Raincatcher Gully, he had gradually picked up some Nymian military tactics and learnt to heal with a lot of difficulty, in case the situation turned dire enough to need more healers. But despite that, and despite the help of his new fairy familiar, still so many people perished that fateless night.

What good had all his efforts done for the Resistance forces of Ala Mhigo? None, absolutely none.

There is still some days before he and the Scions will make the trip to the Far East. He's not sure if his mind welcomes the temporary reprieve, but he knows that physically, he needs it. While he's grateful for any chance to rest, he's not actually sure whether he will be able to do it.

It's dark, and Kotaro thinks that the tranquility, the stars in the sky, and cool breeze should calm his frazzled nerves a little. But yet, even as he listens to the leaves rustle in the gentle breeze and the gentle crashing sounds of the nearby waterfall, it eludes him like a ghost.

He swings his legs off the side of his perch mindlessly, restless.

He's tired, but he can't stop. It's barely six months since the end of the previous war, and now he's caught in the storm of yet another war. How many more times will this happen? How many more times will this cycle repeat? How many more times will he have to endure such trials and tribulations? He's not sure. He's barely had any time to catch his bearings or even realise what he had been suddenly dragged into.

What should he make of Zenos? He finds that he doesn't quite know. He'd been armored from head to toe, like Gaius had been, which revealed nothing about his demeanor. But he was strong, strong enough to knock him out. And his voice, and his choice of words suggested that he was bored. Bored enough to treat them as playthings and leave willy nilly. It was clear that he cared about nothing but entertainment. All that death, just for sport.

Kotaro grits his teeth at the memory of the thoroughly one sided defeat. He'd trained so hard, and used everything in his arsenal, and yet none of it even landed a scratch on Zenos. It was like an ant challenging a mighty lion to a duel and obviously getting thoroughly squashed.

Ever since he became Hydaelyn's champion, he'd fought hordes of Primals. Huge, terrifying entities who could easily make any lesser men their thralls. He'd fought to push the Garlean threat and Ultima off Eorzean soil. He'd fought in the Dragonsong war, facing off a millenia old dragon beset by loss and grief. But nothing had come close to the insanity that was Zenos.

How were they going to win this war if no one, not even him, stood any chance against Zenos? How would they win back Ala Mhigo, and by extension, Doma, the home of his childhood best friend? Alphinaud's plan to force the Garleans to fight a war on two fronts is a smart and strategic idea, but even so, it feels like a lost cause with Zenos in the picture.

"Haurch, what would ye have done?" He wonders aloud defeatedly.

He swirls some aether in his hand, contemplating his options. Nothing he had done so far had been sufficient. Not his skills at tanking, healing, nor offensive attacks. Not that his healing skills were much good - which he thought was strange since he had an affinity for summoning, but yet almost none for Nymian tactics. Both had it's roots in arcanima, and yet despite all their similarities, their minute differences had been enough to stump him.

He had instinctively and prematurely figured how to summon Bahamut, which had been one of the most powerful Primals he had known. But even that had not been enough to turn the tides in their favor - he needed a different kind of egi. And a different kind of strategy.

He starts to hum a melancholic sea shanty, one familiar from his time with the Kraken's Arms.



_I fear not the weather, I fear not the sea_

_I remember the fallen, do they think of me?_

_When their bones in the ocean forever will be_



How had he known how to summon Bahamut in the first place? He had never stopped to contemplate this, but he figures that now is a good time to do so. As he ponders this, familiar words echo in his mind.

_I beg you to defend Eorzea, and guide its people to the future they yet struggle to find_.

… Who were those words from again?

…

Right - it was from the Archon, Louisoix, whose aetherial form they miraculously managed to meet after defeating the primal that he'd been transformed into - the Phoenix. There had been so much aether coalesced into this very being that it'd lingered long enough for one last conversation. The final moments before the aether would disperse enough that he would finally return to the lifestream. He'd met him during his exploration of the Carteneau Flatlands with Alisaie, seeking the truth of the Calamity, which they'd finally managed to glean straight from the mouth of someone involved in the catastophic diasaster that fateful day. And with that, they'd gone on to destroy the primal Bahamut, before it could sap the land of any more of it's aether.

An epiphany hits him. He needed something undying and unwavering. Something that would stand up again and again, no matter how many times it fell. A creature that could heal and shield, and a creature that could also be deadly in it's attacks. He's got his answer now.

As he forms the image in his mind, the aether swirling around his palm gradually changes to a fleeting mix of oranges, reds, and greens, encircling his entire arm. And finally, fiery feathers sprout forth from the left side of his back. Satisfied, he quickly clenches his fist, releasing the aether all at once and letting it dissipate into thin air.

_The flames of everlasting rebirth_.

The Phoenix will always rise from the ashes. _And so will he, in whatever form he needs to be._
