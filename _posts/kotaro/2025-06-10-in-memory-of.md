> 24/8/2025: So, I woke up yesterday morning to the smell of burning paper in the air and had to check my phone for the date of the lunar calendar. After realising what day it was, I started thinking about what Kotaro would do in the context of his childhood friendship. And so, this hastily written fic was born.
{: .note }

![Kotaro looking out at the night sky while sitting under the willow tree](/assets/img/shirogane.jpg)

_Seven hells, why's my nose so itchy?_ Such was Kotaro's first thoughts as he slowly blinked his bleary eyes open, groggy, as the first rays of sunlight filtered through the washi paper of the nearby shoji windows.

It'd been a few months since he had bought this quaint little house in a quiet corner of Shirogane, a residential district near Kugane. He'd chosen it for three reasons. The first, because it was situated right beside a glittering blue water pond filled with koi and shaded by a huge willow tree - the epitome of tranquility that caught his eye. Some days, he'd come back from an adventure and lounge under the tree, drinking sake while staring at the moonlight and the swaying vines of the willow.

The second, it was close to the Ruby Sea. He loved the smell of the salty and humid air. It reminded him so much of home, of Vylbrand, of La Noscea.

Not to mention, there was a market board and a tenkonto (what the Far Easterners called an aetheryte) just by the stairs right behind his house too, making the spot an extremely convenient one for his needs as an adventurer.

After a few more moments of lounging around under the covers, he finally managed to shake free the haze of sleep from his mind. More awake now, he pushed himself up to a sitting position, before swinging his legs off the bed. Now, his mind helpfully supplied the reason for the itch in his nose. The air today was rather… polluted. To be precise, all he could smell was an ashy, burning smell.

Alarm gripping his thoughts, he dashed to his basement to check if anything had caught fire. Fortunately, everything was just as he left it - which really meant barren, because he had so few personal items that there was literally nothing to place down here. _One of these days, he really needed to do something about all that empty space._

Thoughts mired in confusion, he clambered back up, deciding to eat breakfast while pondering where the mystery smell was coming from. Did Hingashi happen to suffer from bouts of extreme weather like in Vylbrand, where some days a maelstrom might kick up? Could a typhoon from the Rasen Kaikyo - the treacherous waters to the east of the Ruby Sea - cause such a smell though? He supposed not. Or had someone accidentally set their own house on fire instead? That, he was certain, could plausibly happen - most houses in the Far East were largely wooden. Good kindling for any spark to catch on really.

Maybe he would find concrete answers if he headed out.

And so that was what he did a bell later, stepping out of his door to take a good look at his surroundings. What he saw surprised him.

There was a gentle grey smoke wafting high into the air from somewhere in the south where the beach was. But that wasn't the only thing he noticed - there was also tiny particles of ash floating in the air around him.

Like a moth drawn to a flame, he climbed down the stairs and started heading to the source of the trail of smoke.

As he neared the source, he spotted a small gathering of Hyurs and a few Auri folk around a mini bonfire of sorts near the beach.

A ritual? He wasn't familiar with far eastern practices nor the meanings they held. How did it compare with some of what they had back in Vylbrand?

Curious, he drew himself closer to observe what was happening. That was when he noticed it - these people were throwing some sort of ceremonial paper into the billowing fire, set atop burning wood which had been nearly stacked and arranged in the shape of a square box.

"What's the occasion today?" He casually asked a participant who had happened to turn around at that moment and noticed him.

The Hyur's eyebrows rose, and he put a hand on his chin, in deep contemplation, before he finally answered.

"Every first day of the fourth astral moon, we come together to honor the deceased." The Hyur began. Then, pointing to the ceremonial paper in his hands, he continued. "We burn these joss paper to symbolize the material possessions we wish to send to them, in hopes that it'll make their journey in the afterlife more comfortable."

Understanding dawned. Kotaro had never really thought about what comes after death. Sure, Lominsans mourned the dead by sending a few sprigs of Chamomile flowers and ashes off the waves, then reciting the Sailor's Requiem. But that was it - as much as farming was a mainstay in La Noscea, with the region producing varieties of fruits and vegetables, folks simply did not offer these to the dead. Kotaro supposes that it was for practical reasons. That being, the land hadn't been as bountiful since the Calamity. Not that he would know, he'd never attended any sort of memorial service before the Calamity.

But even so, this sort of consideration of loved ones well-being even when they've passed? That display of care and loyalty by the Easterners moved the depths of his heart.

His thoughts drew back to the very childhood friend who had inspired his entire adventuring journey so far.

Ryuutaro used to live around these parts, didn't he? Would that not mean that he would have once believed in or partook in similar rituals? Would he like it if Kotaro offered him something, just to show that he remembered him and he was occasionally at the forefront of his thoughts?

_Guess it couldn't hurt._

"Say, would it be alright if I joined? I'm new to this but… there's someone I'd like to honor too." Kotaro responded.

The Hyur nodded with a kindly smile, and handed a stack of the joss papers to him. Carefully, he added them to the bonfire, just like how he'd seen the others do. Mesmerised, he watched as the paper caught the wisps of flames, slowly turning grey, then black, as it curled and crumbled into ash and floated off into the sky, carried by the slight breeze of the sea.

_Ryuutaro, I hope you're living a comfortable life wherever you are. It took me too long but, I've finally found your homeland. It's beautiful, like you've mentioned. I bought a house here too._

_I wish you could have been here to see it._
