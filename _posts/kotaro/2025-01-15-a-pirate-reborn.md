_Hear... Feel.... Think..._

Kotaro blinked in surprise. One moment he was out sailing back from The Cieldalaes, admiring the vastness of the surrounding waters, and the next, he was transported into the middle of nowhere. Floating and surrounded by barren darkness, a small wick of light illuminated a cloaked figure hovering above him. The sinister aura that radiated off the figure gave him the chills - who in Llymlaen's waters was that?

But before he had the time to process it, a large burst of light burst forth around him. When it dispersed, he found himself cloaked in a set of gear he'd never seen himself wear in his life. Robes? Him, a mage? But wouldn't that make him an adventurer of some sorts in this ... dream? Controlled like a puppet, his limbs suddenly moved as if on instinct, shooting a burst of light magic towards the unknown intruder.

He blinked again, and just like that, he found himself back on the stern of the ship he was on, the salty wind tugging at his bandana and his shirt's collar. He dropped his gaze to his hands and stared at them. What in the seven hells was that? A vision? A memory? A warning? A sign of something to come?

A maelstrom of thoughts whirled in his mind. On one hand, he did enjoy the camaradie of this crew. Everyday aboard this vessel ha different challenge, and he never lacked for something new to do or learn. But the deeper he dug down the rabbit hole of his thoughts, the more he realised that something small had been nagging at him. What was it?

This time, a memory - one that he knew well - surged forth, unbidden. A child-like voice from the past, clear as day:

_"Let's visit my homeland someday. I promise it's beautiful."_

Ryuutaro. He hadn't thought of him much since the proceeding sennights after the Calamity. After he'd given up hope on ever finding him again. But, he remembered their promise, made in the glow of the setting sun and salty sea breeze.

For all of his travels in the past few years, he'd never seen much other than the sea. It was with startling clarity that he realised that this needed to change. The sea might bring its own comfort of a sorts, but he realised what he was missing now - the thrill of doing something, completely, radically different. _And the need to fulfill a promise made in the distant past._

He thinks about the vision again - did he really want to be an adventurer? Slowly turning all possibilities in his head, he realised that he had three options. Continue as he was now, figure out what he needed to do to become nunh, or... risky as it sounded, become an adventurer, like what he had just seen in his so called vision. 

_How much more risky was being an adventurer than a privateer, after all?_ he reasoned with himself.

Kotaro tried to imagine himself as nunh - it failed spectacularly and he instantly blanched at the thought of it. Too much duty and traditional hierarchies to follow. Not to mention, he couldn't imagine staying at any single place for decades at a time or taking care of an entire tribe of people. The Seekers who had settled in La Noscea since the year 980 were those who shunned the rigidity of tribal life after all, mainly living scattered, freestyle lives.

An adventurer then? Something about it resonated with him, not that he was entirely sure what. And, it also seemed like the best way to eventually find the homeland of his childhood friend, at least, without skipping all the other wonders of the world in between. Considering this, he finally made his own conclusion.

Resolve hardened, he looked out at the horizon, seeing a coastline in the distance. The ship would reach port in a bell or so, which meant that now was the perfect timing for him to speak with his captain. Kotaro strode up to the starboard of the ship, where the person he needed was looking at the sky with a single-handed binoculars.

"Cap'n. I’d like to have a word with ye..."

Carvallain lowered the binoculars, turned and gave him a skeptical look, but tilted his head, encouraging him to continue.

"Been thinkin'... All these years sailin' with ye, it's been a hell of a ride. But..." Kotaro scratches his chin nervously. "Got the itch t' strike out on me own, see what the realm's got waitin' beyond the sea. I owe ye plenty, an' I'll not soon forget it. But it's time I chased me a new tale. Next tide, I'm thinkin o' settin' out as an 'venturer."

His captain stared at him incredulously for a good long moment, as if he'd suddenly sprouted wings on his head. His eyes searching his face with his sharp and piercing gaze - the one that always seemed to see through his thoughts. Finally, he let out a slow, reluctant sigh, turning his gaze out toward the sea, seemingly looking at nothing.

"Can't say I didn't see this coming. You've been gazing past the waves like there's more beyond the horizon. Go. You have my blessing."

Ack, was he that easy to read...? Or had he just not realised what he had wanted sooner?

A pause. Then he continued.

"But before that... a word of advice. You'll want to speak to Baderon Tenfingers. Proprietor of the Drowning Wench. He'll get you registered with the Adventurers' Guild. He's who you want on your side should you get into any spot of trouble."

"And… Should the sea ever call to you again... you know where to find me." That all too knowing smirk on his face, as if he had him completely in the palm of his hand. _Terrifyin'_, that look. But that was Carvallain.

And Kotaro? Well, he couldn't help himself. "Providin' ye don't end up in Leviathan's belly afore then, aye?" he shot back with a crooked grin, all cheek and no bite.

All he got in response was a refined scoff.

_Tooooooooot._

The signal of their arrival in port. That was it then, time to put his plans into motion.

--

"Greetin's to ye, lad." Baderon smiled as Kotaro walked up to the counter, eyeing his hands. _He really has ten fingers_, he thought absentmindedly.

"Cap'n Carvallain sent me yer way. Lookin' t' sign on as an 'venturer…"

Baderon's brows raised in surprise. But without missing a beat, he started rummaging behind the counter, pulling out a stack of papers and a well-used inkwell, all while continuing to engage in conversation.

"Carvallain, eh? Now _that's_ a rare one. But good thing ye came. There's been a spate o' kidnappin's, see, an' ain't nobody got a clue who's behind 'em. We always need all the help we can get, but 'venturers 'ave a knack fer findin' trouble, see. An' that's why we're 'here to provide any aid we can give."

Kidnappings? That was new to him.

He quickly placed the right forms and quill in front of Kotaro, clearly having done this many times before. "Right then, let's get ye sorted. You just need t' fill this... and that..." his voice guiding Kotaro as he quickly scribbled all the information he needed.

Before he knew it, they were done.

"Me thanks to ye, an' to the Navigator an' all! I knew She'd steered ye my way fer a reason."

Now, time to head home and inform his mother of his plans.

--

"An adventurer?" his mother turned and looked at him curiously. However, she did not stop him - she hadn't stopped him from doing anything he wanted to do since he was born. He's not sure whether that's a good thing, her laissez-faire and mostly hands-off parenting, but he'll just have to take it for what it is.

"Well, you'll need to know some self-defence..." his mother tapped her hand to her chin thoughtfully, like she always did when she was thinking.

She was right, knowing how to operate a cannon or a saw or a bootknife wouldn't be enough to save oneself out on the road. The thought caused him to instinctively reach into his boot for the tiny blade. He pulled it out, swirling it in his fingers while taking a good long moment at it. It was perfectly good steel, in his own opinion. He's not sure if he could quickly kick the habit of reaching for his bootknife in a pinch. It was, afterall, handy and stealthy, and thus absolutely great for catching an enemy off-guard on a good day. A slight of hand that could only be used once - anyone who saw this trick once would never fall for it again. But it was great way to easily throw someone's tempo off in a jiffy. It would not do much damage given its size and Kotaro's terrible aiming skills - but it did its job just fine, according to Kotaro's own standards. He quickly sheathes the object in his hand back into his boot.

Now, he thinks about his bare hands. Should he give hand to hand combat another go? He'd attempted once while still in the Kraken's Arms. But after almost breaking his arm once-

It was then that his mother's voice snapped him out of his musings.

"... I know! You should sign up at the Arcanist Guild. At least Rhid can watch over you!" She clapped her hands together, clearly pleased at her own idea.

He furrowed his brows in confusion. Why was his mother so enthusiastic? He couldn't understand. But he opted not to think on it any further. She probably made a good point, in any case. Plus, magicks had the advantage of range, which he found he rather liked the idea of - standing at a distance and firing a projectile before something knew what hit them. Sounded stealthy enough to him. And, aiming a magical projectile should be easier than aiming an arrow or a throwing knife… right?

Anyroad, the sun was setting now, and he wanted to eat and get to bed. Guess he'll head over to the Arcanist's Guild fresh and early the next morning.

--

"K'tiqa! What brings you here? According to my calculations, your probability of arrival was zero percent." A familiar young lass with pink braided hair and dark blue robes bounced up to him the moment he pushed open the doors to the guild.

"I'm lookin' t' learn the ways o' arcanima…" he responded, unsure how else to word his intent. Luckily, K'lyhia seemed to understand what he wanted to do.

"In that case, Murie over here will get you setup with the paperwork and some basic tools you need. Afterwhich, you can begin some basic training with Thubyrgeim over there." the girl pointed him towards a counter with a petite hyur woman, who bowed politely to him, and started taking out the necessary papers.

Kotaro looked around, then frowned.

"Say K'lyhia, mother said Uncle K'rhid would be 'ere...?"

"The Guildmaster…?" K'lyhia's composure seemed to crack for a split second, but instantly vanished the next moment. Shrugging her shoulders, she continued. "Unfortunately, he hasn't showed up at the guild for a good while. But don't you worry, between me and Thubyrgeim, you're hundred percent sure to be in good hands."

_Bugger. So much fer tribal relations_, Kotaro thought dryly, ears flicking in mild annoyance, as he nodded mutely at K'lyhia's explanation.
