"Still in Limsa, are ye, Kotaro?"

Kotaro's ears swished and perked up at hearing Baderon's voice.

" 'Ow'd ye fancy a trip outside the city walls? I'd like ye to visit an orchard by the name o' Summerford Farms. The owner, Staelwyrn, is an old mate o' mine. 'E's got 'imself a crew o' mostly reformed pirate types, if ye can believe that, and 'as been tryin' to bring a bit o' life back to the fields after the Calamity ruined the soil. 'E mentioned needin' another 'and or two, and yer name sprang to mind."

Reformed pirate types? Was that possible for a pirate to trade their scallywag days for the fields? Even if they weren't the bloodthirsty types, many had preferred the thrill of sailing in waters fraught with the unknowns. _I'd sooner be keelhauled than be locked in a field with crops_, someone once said. Kotaro wasn't quite sure what to understand from Baderon's words as he rode his chocobo across the fields and up to the farm.

Dismounting, he left his chocobo with the stablehands, patting down its deep-indigo feathers, dark and velvety as the depths of the Indigo Deep, before leaving. He slowly headed up to the top to look for Staelwyrn. As he strode through the fields filled with orange orchards, surrounded by gruff, pirate types, it all but confirmed his thoughts. 

_Pirates, but carrying scythes, meant for tilling the fields._ Never in his wildest imaginations did he believe that his thoughts would ring true, and that wasn't a sight he was used to.

But then he observed something else, from the corner of his eye. All but one of the pirates was dressed in red garb. Why wasn't he dressed like the others? Furthermore, he had a sullen expression plastered to his face, a slightly disturbed look in his eyes as he brushed past. 

_A newcomer? But something doesn't quite seem right._

Staelwyrn, luckily, as he found, was rather good-natured, despite his imposing and towering look as a Sea Wolf Roegadyn. His request was simple - to look into the Seasong Grotto, where he believed some unsavory fellows with azure-tattooed faces had arrived from, and been spying on his yeomen.

Mounting his trusty chocobo once more, he made his way to the Seasong Grotto, strangely meeting zero resistance. He frowned in confusion, wondering if Staelwyrn had somehow gotten his information mixed up, or he'd simply been too late. There was nothing suspicious at the Grotto, other than a monument right at the center. With nothing to lose, he stopped to read the words on it.

```
    I am the waves that bear.
    I am the winds that guide.
    I am the evening stars.
    I am the morning sky.
    I am born of the sea.
    And there shall I die.
```

He recognised it at once - the Sailors' Requiem.

  
A dirge for not just for the dead, but for the living as well - a song of protection for those adrift, and a gentle guide for departed souls to find their way back to the sea. _Melancholic, yet serene._ There was some sort of peace just reading it. And well, that was it. With nothing else left to do, Kotaro turned, ready to head back to the farms, when he heard a soft voice behind him.

"Ah, you are the adventurer I've seen around Summerford Farms."

A sun-kissed Miqo'te, with short white hair, and teal eyes glinting with mischievousness. Before she could continue however, a towering gobbue rushed in, startling them both. It was frantic, frenzied, and angry. Something was wrong. Gobbues were usually calm and avoid places with people. Something must have triggered it.

"As I suspected, the aetheric disturbance here is no natural occurrence. Let us attend to the task at hand, unpleasant though it be."

An aetheric disturbance? He couldn't sense anything, at least not with his eyes. He suspected that the strange contraption hanging from neck of his newly met companion could have been the key. Maybe she was a scholarly type - she did give off the impression of having knowledge of eld.

They made quick work of the gobbue. As it fell, Kotaro spied a sea blue crystal it dropped on the ground near it. _Curious_, he thought, as he picked it up and turned it in his hands. At first, nothing happened. And suddenly, he was swept away by the darkness again.

"Hear... Feel... Think..."

_That voice again._

He slowly blinked open his eyes and looked around. This time, he was in front of a giant mass of a crystal.

Hydaelyn, it introduced itself. And it gave him a mission - to journey and claim the crystals to banish the darkness. To deliver the world from its fate. 

_Great, I'm now an errand-runner fer the gods?_ he thought as the scene faded from his eyes.

And he came back to the world of the living, blinking his eyes in confusion.

"Awake again, are we? I beg your pardon? A "towering crystal"? I'm sure I don't-- Oh. Well, well... This has been a day of unexpected revelations." his companion responded in surprise as he revealed to her what had just happened.

This scholar clearly seemed to have knowledge of what this crystal was. He wondered then and there if he would ever be able to get her to impart that knowledge to him. But, in light of what had just happened at the grotto, it was clearly not the time to discuss his ill-timed vision.

"I must continue my investigation. I found this blade in the goobbue's back. I suggest you deliver this knife to your patron, along with a warning concerning the pirates' probable involvement in this murky business. May our paths cross again under the light of the Crystal."

--

Staelwyrn looked none too pleased to hear the news that Kotaro reported back. It was all of his worst suspicions confirmed, afterall.

But first, he had a bigger problem to deal with - one of his charges selling their oranges, meant for someone else, to the _goblins_. Sevrin, he was called. Easily recognized by his red garb. _It was that guy he walked past the first time he'd come to the farms,_ Kotaro thought as understanding dawned on him.

He was nothing but trouble. The moment danger reared its head, he bolted, leaving his henchmen to fend for themselves. They'd been furious, feeling utterly betrayed.

"The way he flew out of here, I was sure he'd sold us down the river!"

"Would've gotten away meself if not fer Sevrin shovin' me aside."

Pirates may be rough around the edges, but they followed codes. And camaradie had been a big part of why Kotaro had stayed all those years back. Bonds forged from weathering the unpredictable and stormy tides together - something you couldn't do as a single person. A flare of anger shot through Kotaro at the way Sevrin had treated his comrades.

But all Sevrin had to say for himself?

"You saved me mates, and for that, I owe you. S'pose I should be ashamed I couldn't clean up my own mess...But a pirate knows no shame!"

_Unrepentant, the bastard._ But there was something in his eyes, a flicker of relief. As much as he hid it behind his bravado, he wasn’t entirely without remorse. Still, it was clear that he played by his own rules, with little care for the honor others swore to uphold. He was a disgrace to them all, but Kotaro found that he couldn't hold onto his temper, and it sizzled into dust.

He supposed that he did understand. It wasn't hard to see how a man like Sevrin had come to think the way he did, not especially after he'd seen how his eyes looked the very first time he brushed past. Living a life on the streets of Limsa Lominsa, where you might have to steal for even scraps, and where every decision was a gamble, had a way of hardening you. Kotaro had seen it before - how survival twisted you, shaped you into someone who didn't think twice about abandoning others if it meant saving yourself. Sevrin might be a scoundrel, but Kotaro couldn't quite begrudge him for it. Scars etched deep into the soul were difficult to heal from, and Kotaro saw that in Sevrin, even if he'd never admit it.

The problem didn't end there. The very next day, when he arrived at Summerford Farms, he found Staelwyrn pacing around at his usual spot, agitated. He was normally level headed and calm - something had clearly shaken him to the core.

"Staelwyrn, what's eatin' at ya?" Kotaro asked, eyeing him with concern.

Staelwyrn hesitated, clearly reluctant to give voice to his thoughts. A flicker of doubt crossed his face.

"What I'd ask of you now goes beyond aught I've asked before. Would you hear my plea all the same?"

Kotaro nodded, giving Staelwyrn his full attention.

"I've grown accustomed to his insolence, but somethin' tells me this is different. It ain't exactly damnin', but when last I had words with him, he didn't look defiant so much as hunted."

_Hunted,_ Kotaro thought. If Staelwyrn hadn't sensed Sevrin's unease days ago, then what he'd seen in his eyes today was far worse - something deeper, darker, that even Staelwyrn hadn't fully grasped. That couldn't be good.

"The ungrateful little cur bites me every chance he gets, but I can't quite bring myself to cut him loose. Suffice it to say, I'd rather he didn't come to a sticky end."

And neither did Kotaro. He might be a backstabbing fool, but his gut feeling told him that Sevrin wasn't inherently a bad person deep down.

"Aye, I'll track him down," Kotaro replied, his tone steady and resolute.

He ran off, tracking every possible lead, and moving as fast as he could, until it led him to Woad Wisper Canyon. And that's where he saw the familiar faces - faces he'd saved just the day before.

The group Staelwyrn had been chasing - aggressive pirates with faces marked by swirling azure tattoos, their eyes burning with a feral, predatory anger. But what caught Kotaro off guard was Sevrin, standing at the forefront, facing off with them, his posture one of defiance, as if guarding the very people he'd once abandoned. _A change of heart?_ Kotaro couldn’t believe it. Still, he knew one thing for sure: he couldn't just walk away now.

"Few are they who betray the Serpent Reavers twice. You will not do so a third time!" A voice rang out, harsh and unforgiving, to Sevrin.

The truth hit Kotaro like being suddenly smashed into a wall of bricks. _So, that was the story._ That was what drove Sevrin to desperation.

The Serpent Reavers - well known to many Lominsans. Zealots, fanatics, thralls to Leviathan - the great serpent of the sea they worshipped with a fervor that bordered on madness. _And they were brutal._ To cross paths with them was to tempt death itself. Sevrin had been entangled far too deep with them to truly escape their grasp. No wonder one of his henchmen had made the remark of rather bowing to a them than be in Sevrin's debt - if these reavers didn't kill you, they would break you in ways unfathomable.

Unfortunately, as soon as they spotted Kotaro, the Reavers wasted no time and made a swift, hasty escape, vanishing into the cracks of the canyon. In their wake, they left behind a massive, magical golem - an imposing stone behemoth, its eyes glowing with an eerie light.

Kotaro wasted no time in sending his carbuncle out with a sic. The battle raged on, each blow from the golem sending tremors through the ground, but Kotaro held his ground, K'lyhia's motto echoing through his mind - use strategy to manipulate one's situation into the desired reality. 

_Wear it down with damage over time,_ he thought, refreshing Miasma and Bio every minute or so, their poison working its way slowly but surely on the golem. His carbuncle landing Downburst with every whip of its tail. Magicks clashing against stone, sweat beading on his brow as the fight dragged on. At last, with a final, decisive burst of Fester, the golem crumbled to the earth, leaving nothing but a lumpy pile of stone and a deafening silence in its wake.

--

As Sevrin told his sorry tale, all Staelwyrn could do was stand there in shock, the weight of Sevrin's words settling heavily, disbelief mixing with something darker.

"Seven hells... Reavers may look and sound and act like pirates, but they're naught but the fishbacks' flunkies."

But all that's well ends well. Sevrin, finally free from the chains of his past and the guilt that had haunted him, decided to turn himself in to the Yellowjackets. It wasn't just Staelwyrn who felt a flicker of relief; Kotaro, too, found a quiet sense of satisfaction in seeing Sevrin unshackled and ready to face the consequences of his actions. His eyes, shining with clear determination for once. After all the chaos, it was rare to see someone take responsibility for their misdeeds - and in that moment, Kotaro had nothing but respect for him.
