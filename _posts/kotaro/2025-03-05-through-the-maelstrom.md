Kotaro heaved a deep, world-weary sigh. He should have expected it, but the reality was, he hadn't. After these many encounters with various groups of Serpent Reavers and Sahagins, of course, who should he face but none other than their mighty lord of the whorls, Leviathan?

_"May the Navigator guide and keep you, 'til the seas swallow all."_

Steeling himself for the battle head, the words of the Maelstrom officer rang in his ears as the ship pulled away from the shore.

He may have been a pirate once, but even the most seasoned sailors harbored a smidge of ingrained fear of the sea, no matter how much they loved the vast body of water. _Especially when today was one such day where it was prowled by a giant sea serpent, wrecking havoc in all ways imaginable._

The sea - vast enough to offer boundless freedom.  
The sea - deeper still, with dangers enough to humble any man.  
On a choppy day, waves huge enough to swallow them all, as if representing its bottomless anger.

The thought of facing the monstrous sea beast that was Leviathan was nothing short of terrifying. It wasn't just the sheer size or fury of the creature that chilled him — it was also the fact that it was a Primal, a being given form from the Sahagins' zealous devotion. This was his first and only chance to bring it down, and the weight of that truth pressed on him like the depths of the Indigo Deep itself. Failing which, the entire port of Limsa Lominsa would be swallowed by tidal waves created by none other than this giant serpent itself. Not that he was alone though - he was with a party of seven other adventurers, all with some sort of protection against tempering.

The battle, unfortunately, started off on the wrong foot.

A sudden slap of Leviathan's tail sent the entire platform lurching, knocking him off balance and causing him to accidentally aim his Fester spell towards the tail. He realised too late that the tail had a magicked barrier, as he got hit by the double whammy of slamming hard into the railings and the force of his spell being flung back into his face. By Llymlaen's teats, this was going to bruise to the seven hells.

As if that wasn't enough, he'd strayed too close to the tank and got slapped by a massive water bubble straight to the face - knocking the wind out of him and dragging a good chunk of his focus with it. It left him somewhat disoriented and groggy for a good minute or so, before he could finally catch his bearings.

Casting had never been harder - it was hard enough to keep track of Bio and Miasma, the staple of any summoning kit, and even harder to cast his Ruin spells. The platform pitched and bucked with every rock of the waves, threatening to throw off his aim with every pulse of the sea. How was he to form any strategy to defeat Leviathan like this?

And the worst part? His carbuncle staring at him almost judgementally with its beady eyes, awaiting his every command. _Right, he'd forgotten he could simply yell "Sic 'em, Carbuncle!"…_

Just then, Leviathan summoned Gyre Spumes, and some Sahagins leapt onto the platform. Dealing with the Sahagins were a simple enough affair, but the Gyre Spumes? They were a fucking pain - they burst and popped with a spray of saltwater that agitated his tail and left an annoying itchiness on his skin.

But before he could completely get over the Gyre Spumes, Leviathan suddenly shifted tactics. With a low, guttural roar, it dove beneath the waves — then came surging across the platform in violent bursts, its massive body slicing through the arena like a water jet. The attacks came fast and unpredictable, keeping everyone scrambling to stay on their feet, the unlucky ones slipping around as water sploshed past them.

For a moment, he stood frozen, trying to make sense of the sudden change. Then, over the crashing sounds of water and shouts of confusion, a single voice cut through the chaos.

**"The shield! Activate the shield! Now!"**

The tone was confident, clear and sharp with urgency. It probably came from one of the veteran adventurers, someone whose battle instincts had been forged and honed through countless battles.

That snapped him into motion. He scrambled to yank the only lever he could see at the edge of the platform, hoping that was the right thing to do - he hadn't been paying attention to instructions back on shore! And luckily, he was just in time too - as a shimmering barrier flickered to life, a roaring whirlpool surged around them, threating to break the platform apart. Never had he been more relieved for the existence of arcane magicks. Yet even amidst the maelstrom, he found himself captivated by the mesmerizing beauty of the water swirling in strong, radiant arcs around him.

_I could dream o' this everyday_, Kotaro thought to himself absentmindedly, momentarily losing track of everything happening around him.

Thankfully, Leviathan didn’t unleash any more surprise tactics after that. But wearing it down was a slow, grueling ordeal, and exhaustion was setting in fast. Everyone was running on fumes. Morale was dropping. Preventable mistakes started occurring. Then, just as it seemed that everyone was on the brink of collapse, Leviathan let out a piercing screech — and in an instant, its massive form dissipated into a spray of droplets, released into thin air.

It was over. _It was over._ That's all that mattered.

He was exhausted, and all he wanted to do was catch up on a good long nap and drink his fill at Baderon's. No more seafaring adventures for the next week or so.

And hopefully, this was it - no more having to deal with fishbacks and their flunkies. There'd been too much encounters with their nonsense on Vylbrand the past few months to last a lifetime.
