"Say lad, ye interested in helping me out with summat?"

Kotaro looked up from his drink to the owner of the voice. Baderon, the proprietor of the Drowning Wench. He'd been coming to his tavern daily for a drink ever since being introduced to him. While he was grateful for all his help in starting his adventuring career, this was the first time he'd gotten a direct request from him, rather than a request passed on from someone else. It was surprising, to say the least.

"Word 'as it some suspicious lookin' buggers 'ave been sneakin' in an' out o' the Sastasha Seagrot, an' I need a trustworthy 'venturer to 'ead up north of Aleport an' find out who they are an' what they're up to."

Sastasha? Wasn't it just a cave? 

But as Baderon continued to explain, he had the feeling it was much more than that. And strangely, the prospect of it excited him. If not a cave, then what lurked there, waiting to be explored? Shimmering shoals of seashells hoarded by pirates? Sahagins? Or something else entirely...? The possibilities were endless.

"Reckon I’ll also throw a few more 'venturers yer way, aye. Safer in a party, less chance o’ bein’ shark bait out there."  

"Aye, I'll give it a look." Kotaro responded easily, eager to put his recently learnt arcanima skills to the test. With the map given to him in hand, he quickly made his way to the western shores of  La Noscea.   

--

As he sat outside the entrance of the Seagrot, waiting for his party to fill, he listened to stories of other adventurers who'd been in the trade longer than he had. 

_"Our journeys are not ended by the attainment of riches or fame. It is an unquenchable thirst for the unknown that drives us into the wilds time and again."_

_"We travel where the wind takes us, and drink in all the wonders this world has to offer. Only an adventurer can understand the joy of such freedom -- of discovering hidden places and experiencing sights and sounds that few may ever know."_

Their description of an adventurer's life sounded so romantic - as if the call of the unknown was something that unbound the very depths of one's soul. That being truly alive was living in the moment, chasing the next big thing. Was this the affirmation he needed? That this was his calling in life?   

The voice of the sea had called to him years ago - a song that sang of tides that rose and fell on a whim, bringing great reverence and yet fear. Its salty air carrying comfort yet tragedy. It could be their friend, and it could also be their greatest enemy. In its embrace, he had found boundless joy, and in its moods, he learnt wisdom.   

But what if he could go much further than that, and explore the very depths that this world had to offer? He hummed softly as he considered everything that had happened so far.  

Soon, he was brought out of his musings by a dark skinned Miqo'te who had silently walked past him to take a seat at the sheltered goods. Judging by the robes and cane, Kotaro guessed he might be a healer before bounding over to recruit him.   

"Hey there, name’s Kotaro. Ya’ ‘ere on Baderon’s request?”   

There was a flinch at the sound of his voice –maybe he spoke a little too loudly for the other– as green blue heterochromatic eyes met his own red yellow ones wearily. The other Miqo’te stared at him without a word for a pause, the silence between them turning awkward even by his own standards. 

That awkward tension dissipated when he finally responded, “Yes, he mentioned there might be someone here already. My name is Mikh'to.”   

Kotaro clapped both of his palms together in a loud slap.

“Right then - two more an’ we’re set!" Kotaro grinned cheerily. 

Mikh'to strode forward and sat beside him, both of them lounging together in a short comfortable silence. Kotaro broke the silence first.

"Say, ye from 'round these parts? Ain’t seen a Miqo’te like yers wanderin' about 'ere before."  

"No, I'm from Gridania."  

"Gridania, ye say? What’s the place like, all trees an' whispers?" he asked curiously.

"It's a dense greatwood, yes. You're not entirely wrong about the whispers - the elementals always speak, at least to those who know what to listen out for. Offend the woods, and even the trees will turn against you. And so most people there respect the forest."

"Seven hells, really now? Guess those rumors were true. Ye talk like ye experienced it firsthand yerself - summat ye learn as a conjurer?"  

Mikh'to nods.

They continued bantering for another good bell before the party finally filled, and they entered the seagrot, with much trepidation and excitement.

The cave was dark, but lit up by the glow of otherworldly bushes all around. Kotaro could only gaze in wonder as he jogged past them all while swatting away bats - they were so pretty. So mesmerized was he, that Mikh'to had to yank on his sleeve. "Oi, pay attention, there seems to be a bloody note here. Guess whoever was here must have left it behind in a hurry."

_"The Capten likes his cabege Green."_

Seemingly useless information? Or a codeword for something else? He wasn't sure. They continued onwards to the next room, racing past all the little balls of flames until they came to a seemingly dead end - at least until the tank spotted an inconspicuous switch, and pressed it. Nothing happened. Maybe it was a false alarm after all?

Mikh'to looked deep in thought, then suddenly announced, "Maybe the corals have something to do with it, the note mentioned something about _green_ just now..."

He turned to the nearby green coral and threw a stone spell, when suddenly, a huge coeurl jumped in, seemingly from thin air.  

"There's something suspicious about this cave for sure, coeurls don't usually live down here," their bard called out, as they scrambled to put it down.  

They tried the switch again, and this time, an opening appeared in the rocks. They ran through it, spotting several shadows and figures ahead. As they neared, Kotaro's eyes narrowed - he recognised the garb, and most importantly, the tattoos. This was about the third time he'd seen them. "Serpent Reavers!" he hissed. _Pirates who were thralls to the primal, Leviathan._ They quickly cut and dashed through every room they could find, the sharp sounds of steel and magic ringing through the air. Every pirate who crossed them fell quickly.  

At last, they reached the final chamber. They froze for a moment as they spotted a towering monstrous form with blue scales and fins, with webbed hands and feet ending in claws. It let out an angry hissing sound, baring its sharp fangs and trident at them. It took them a moment, but with slowly dawning horror, the sight before them finally sank in.  

"They've allied with the Sahagins!"  

"No time to waste - Baderon needs to hear of everything that transpired here, and fast!" the tank growled, charging ahead to face the threat looming before them.  

--  

"Fishbacks?" Baderon raised his brow quizzically, clearly stunned by how far the problem had gone.  "An' there I was thinkin' ye'd only 'ave to crack a pirate skull or three. Seven hells... Ye turn yer back fer five bloody minutes, an' the scaly bastards've set up shop on yer bleedin' doorstep..."

He pondered for a while, deep in thought, as he handed over a bag of gil to their party for their troubles. Finally, after a long moment, he spoke again.

"Now, I'll bet yer dyin' fer a rest, but just in case yer not, I was wonderin' if ye might be interested in takin' on another job? It just so 'appens that I've 'ad a request from our sister guild in Gridania, see..."  

Gridania? Had Baderon really just mentioned Gridania? He could not believe his ears. He had never set foot in the Twelveswood, but now, standing beside a fellow adventurer who knew its ins and outs, the name stirred something deep within him. He pictured towering oak trees swaying in a whispering breeze. What the woods would look like with the soft morning light breaking through the canopy. The scent of moss and earth thick in the air. The thoughts gripped him with an itch.  

The sea would always call to him, but beyond its horizon, the world was vast. Distant lands, strange customs, untold wonders - he wanted to see it all.

Everything else that Baderon had to say to them all fell on deaf ears. But it wasn't important - Kotaro was sure that Mikh'to would have the braincell to remember important details for him. "Mikh’to, yer from Gridania, aye? Come on then — show me the place! Bet it’ll be a right good time!"
