To say that Captain Carvallain was strange was the understatement of the century.

His speech was surprisingly refined for a pirate - always measured, thoughtful, and entirely devoid of any hints of Lominsan or even strings of crude curses, always preferring euphemisms - it was clear that he'd had a strict upbringing, probably as a lord of a mansion.

Not only that, he was adventurous, and had foresight too. In a way that he'd never seen in any other pirate. Pirates typically never played by the books, but Carvallain was the living embodiment of a radical revolutionary. Neither Hyllfyr nor Rhoswen could compare with him - the former was probably on his deathbed and the latter only seemed to care about chasing the glories of the past.

But that was precisely what drew Kotaro to join the Kraken's Arms. To scratch an itch, a curiosity. The maddening need to see just how far this idealist in a captain's coat could go - and how many people he'd drag to the seven hells with him. He knew he played a dangerous whimsical game, but the best moments in life were always found from the high of living in the moments, wasn't it?

Kotaro had been working as a shipwright in Limsa Lominsa for a few summers - steady pay, honest labor - but the urge to journey beyond the shores of Vylbrand was becoming stronger with each turn of the season.

And unfortunately, armed with questionable wisdom and an even worse timing in his head, he finally answered call of the sea, _like a fool chasing a siren's song._ Which idiot in their right mind would make a sudden job switch right after a hell's besotted calamity, when the whole world was upturned from its events? _Kotaro, of course._

_Maybe it was just an excuse. To convince himself that by sailing beyond the shores of the familiar, he might chance upon Ryuutaro. It didn't matter if he was chasing a ghost. His closest childhood friend. A refugee from the war in the Far Eastern lands. Missing for weeks now, gone without a trace. Vanished into thin air admist the chaos of the Calamity._

Either that or, he supposed it might be the instincts that Sun Seekers had inherited from their ancestors.

It's not to say that he had completely traded away an honest life for the life of a turncoat. After all, _these were privateers sanctioned by the thalassocracy itself_.

For those who knew nothing of Limsa Lominsa's storied history, it would look like any respectable city, especially across the roiling waves of the Rhotano Sea. But little did they know how wrong they were - it was haven for bandits, cutthroats and all sorts of under-handed characters - provided one knew which nook or cranny to look. It had, in many ways, always been a city run by pirates. Except, guided by a code that had but 3 simple rules meant to keep them united against the threat in the North:

1.  No crew would cheat another of its plunder.
    
2.  No crew would rob a fellow Lominsan.
    
3.  No crew would sell fellow men into slavery.
    

When Merlwyb became the Admiral, that code was further extended to protect all members of the Eorzean Alliance, not just the Lominsans, in the face of the greater threat from the Garleans. Known as the Galadion Accord, the three great pirate crews who signed them - the Kraken's Arms, the Sanguine Sirens, and the Bloody Executioners - became officially recognised as privateers.

And Carvallain, with his quick wit and shrewd mind for strategy, quickly pivoted his business towards what he called the _spice trade_. It was simply a clever working around of the treaty, making everything they did perfectly legal. To say that he had a flair for politics was calling it short - it was clear as Llymlaen's waters that he had training, instinct, and possibly an innate talent for it. Kotaro sometimes suspected he could charm a poor Maelstrom officer into selling their top-secret intelligence if he so wanted to. After all, any opportunity to earn gil, however unorthodox it was, was simply his top priority.

But where could he have possibly originated from, to be so effortlessly sharp at politics and speech? Not to mention, he was also stupidly good at reading the stars, almost as if he had a knack for astrology. By elimination, it left him with two places - Sharlayan or Ishgard. Sharlayan, he ruled out immediately - he didn't seem to be the bookish type that loved to talk theories day in and out. That left Ishgard. He'd never actually met an Ishgardian - they were so loathe to leave their lands, caught up in their own problems as it was. But he'd heard drunken tales - something about dragons, and possibly star worship? Or maybe that was just another tavern rumor gone wild? Either way, it made about as much sense as anything else.
